## Messaging Foundation

**Site-level framework:** StoryBrand (SB7)

**Why this framework for this project:**
This is a trust-heavy local service business where the buyer must believe in the guide before they will act — they are handing over the keys to everything that keeps their organization running. StoryBrand casts the business manager as the hero and Jeremy as the experienced guide, which matches how Pinnacle actually sells: referrals, an on-site visit, and a relationship that renews monthly rather than a contract that locks people in. The customer's before/after transformation — from accidental IT manager to confident operator — is the core of the pitch, which is exactly what SB7 is built to carry.

**The core narrative (5 bullets):**
- **The customer is:** a business manager, owner, CFO, or operations manager at a 10–80 person company or a nonprofit in northeast Indiana, who became the IT person by accident and has been holding it together with effort and luck.
- **Their problem is:** a patchwork of vendors who each own one narrow slice, never look at the whole picture, and are impossible to reach — so nobody is watching for threats, nobody knows what they're paying for, and nobody knows what's about to fail.
- **The cost of inaction is:** reactive IT doesn't prevent crises, it just responds after the damage is done. Every day without someone watching is another day their team, clients, and data are exposed — and the chaos costs real money even when nothing looks broken.
- **We help by:** being the one team that owns all of it — network, devices, phones, cyber security, cameras and door access — watched every day, with a real person who answers the phone and no long-term contract required.
- **Success looks like:** their organization runs as professionally behind the scenes as it does out front, and they get back to running the business instead of chasing vendors.

**The villain:** fragmented, reactive IT.

**Section-level formulas:**

| Section | Formula | Notes |
|---------|---------|-------|
| Hero | PAS → promise | The pain is the strongest hook here, but keep it calm. "IT that actually answers the phone" names the pain and the fix in one line. |
| Proof strip | Pure social proof | No formula — real numbers only. Leave placeholders until confirmed. |
| Stakes | PAS (problem + agitate, no solve yet) | Agitate with the three questions, then stop. The solve is the next section. |
| Value proposition | FAB | Four service cards: feature → advantage → benefit, one sentence each. |
| Who we serve | FAB, segmented | Two audiences, same formula, different language. |
| Guide | Empathy + authority | StoryBrand guide beat. Empathy first, then credentials. Never credentials first. |
| Plan | StoryBrand Plan (3 steps) | Keep it simple and sequential. Removes the fear of "what happens if I call?" |
| Lead generator | PAS (sanctioned urgency) | The one place sharper language is allowed. See Open Conflicts #4. |
| Success vs. failure | Two-column contrast | SB7 stakes beat. Failure column is factual, not catastrophic. |
| Explanatory paragraph | Plain SEO prose | For search engines and skimmers who want the literal service list. |
| Final CTA | AIDA (compressed) | Attention + one action. No competing buttons. |

**Voice and tone:**
Write like a trusted local tech who explains things over coffee: clear, calm, specific, and short. The customer is the hero; Pinnacle is the guide who has done this many times. Short sentences, mostly under 20 words. Paragraphs of three sentences or fewer. Headlines describe the client's outcome, not Pinnacle's services.

| Trait | Sounds like | Never sounds like |
|-------|-------------|-------------------|
| Plain-spoken | "Your Wi-Fi drops every afternoon. We'll find out why." | "Leverage optimized network infrastructure." |
| Local | "We're in Fort Wayne and can be on site the same day." | "Serving clients nationwide." |
| Accountable | "One number to call. A real person answers." | "Submit a ticket through our portal." |
| Calm | "Here's what we'd fix first." | Fear tactics about hackers and ransomware. |

**What this site must never sound like:**
Corporate, vague, or hype-driven. No "solutions" as a filler noun, no "passionate about helping you succeed," no "synergy." No acronyms the reader has to decode — MSP, RMM, and EDR rarely belong on this site. No invented statistics or testimonials. And no national-provider polish: this should feel like meeting Jeremy and his team, not a faceless brand.

## Lead Generator (transitional CTA)

A full-width band in a contrasting color, so it reads as a separate offer rather than another section.

- **Heading:** Is Your Business One Click From a Cyber Security Meltdown?
- **Copy:** It only takes one wrong click, one reused password, or one forgotten account that never got shut off. Answer a few quick questions and see where your organization stands before an attacker finds out for you.
- **Button:** Get My Security Score
- **Microcopy under button:** Free. Takes [X] minutes. Results sent instantly.
- **Optional graphic:** security score gauge

The assessment collects an email, returns a score, and gives a list of actions. On its results page, put the **Book an IT Walkthrough** button directly under the action list — anyone with a low score is the warmest lead the site will produce. The nav item for this assessment stays visible on every page; it is the lowest-commitment way in and should never be more than one click away.

## Open Conflicts

> **Four real disagreements between the source documents. Each needs a human decision before build — do not let the vibe coder pick.**

1. **Primary CTA label — three versions exist.** The wireframe says *Book an IT Walkthrough* / *Book a walkthrough*. The homepage copy doc says *Schedule a Tour*. The brand guide says *Book a free IT assessment*. This file uses **Book an IT Walkthrough** throughout because the wireframe is the most recent concrete artifact, but the label appears in the header, hero, plan, final CTA, and assessment results page — pick one and change it everywhere.

2. **Ideal client size.** The BrandScript says 10–80 employees. The brand guide says 10–20. This file uses 10–80 with 10–20 as the sweet spot. It matters for the `/businesses` page copy.

3. **What the proposal looks like.** The wireframe's step 2 promises "a clear recommendation and a flat monthly price." The BrandScript and copy doc promise "two options: one that fixes your most pressing problems now, and one that brings everything up to a standard you've never had." These are different sales motions. The two-option version also implies a 60–90 day remediation promise that the flat-price version doesn't.

4. **Fear in the lead generator vs. the calm voice rule.** The brand guide explicitly lists "fear tactics about hackers and ransomware" as something the brand never sounds like. The lead-generator headline is *Is Your Business One Click From a Cyber Security Meltdown?* Either sanction this as a deliberate exception for the one conversion band (what this file assumes) or rewrite it in the calm register.
